## Overview

UQ Connect is a conceptual redesign of the University of Queensland's student portal, focusing on improving information architecture and streamlining common student tasks. This project was undertaken as a self-directed study to apply IA principles learned in coursework.

The current portal aggregates many essential services but suffers from fragmented navigation and inconsistent interaction patterns, leading to student frustration and support ticket volume.

## Problem

Through preliminary analysis, I identified several key issues:

- Students struggle to find important deadlines and announcements
- Navigation relies heavily on institutional jargon unfamiliar to new students
- Mobile experience is significantly degraded
- Related tasks are scattered across multiple systems

### Problem Statement

> "How might we reorganize the student portal to reduce time-to-task for common activities while maintaining access to the full breadth of university services?"

## Research

### Analytics Review

Working with anonymized usage data, I analyzed:

- Most visited sections and pages
- Common search queries
- Drop-off points in key user flows
- Mobile vs desktop usage patterns

### Card Sorting

I conducted open card sorting with 24 students from various faculties:

- Participants organized 45 portal items into logical groups
- Analysis revealed 6 natural category clusters
- Current navigation matched user mental models only 40% of the time

### Task Analysis

I observed 10 students completing 5 common tasks:

1. Checking exam schedule
2. Enrolling in courses
3. Finding a lecturer's contact information
4. Submitting an assignment
5. Accessing grades

**Key Findings:**
- Average of 4.2 clicks for tasks that should take 2
- 60% of participants used search as primary navigation
- Mobile users abandoned tasks 3x more than desktop

## Insights

### Insight 1: Mental Models Differ by Year

First-year students think in terms of "classes" and "homework," while later-year students use official terminology like "course units" and "assessments." Navigation should accommodate both.

### Insight 2: Time-Sensitive Information Gets Lost

Critical deadlines buried within calendars and announcement feeds. Students rely on unofficial sources (group chats, Reddit) for deadline reminders.

### Insight 3: Mobile is Primary for Quick Tasks

Over 70% of grade checking and schedule viewing happens on mobile, but the responsive design makes these tasks unnecessarily difficult.

## Ideation

### Design Principles

Based on research insights, I established three design principles:

1. **Progressive Disclosure** - Show summary first, details on demand
2. **Action-Oriented** - Lead with what students can do, not what exists
3. **Contextually Aware** - Surface relevant information based on academic calendar

### Information Architecture

I developed a new IA through iterative tree testing:

- Reduced top-level categories from 12 to 6
- Created task-based navigation groupings
- Introduced "Quick Actions" panel for common tasks

### Wireframe Exploration

I created wireframe variations exploring:

- Dashboard-centric vs. search-centric approaches
- Widget customization options
- Notification consolidation strategies

## Prototype

### Dashboard Redesign

The new dashboard features:

- Personalized "Today" view with upcoming deadlines
- Quick action buttons for top 5 student tasks
- Consolidated notification center
- Academic progress visualization

### Navigation Overhaul

Key navigation changes:

- Dual navigation supporting both task-based and category browsing
- Persistent search with intelligent suggestions
- Breadcrumb trails for deep pages
- Mobile-first bottom navigation

### Mobile Experience

Dedicated mobile optimizations:

- Thumb-zone-friendly interaction targets
- Swipe gestures for common actions
- Offline access to schedules and grades
- Native-feeling transitions

## Testing

### Tree Testing

I validated the new IA with 50 participants:

- Findability improved from 58% to 84%
- Average time-to-find reduced by 35%
- Confidence ratings increased from 3.1 to 4.2 out of 5

### Prototype Testing

Moderated testing of the interactive prototype:

- Task success rate: 94% (up from 72%)
- SUS score: 78 (up from 61)
- Participants completed tasks 40% faster on average

### A/B Testing Simulation

I designed an A/B testing plan for potential implementation:

- Metrics: task completion rate, time on task, support ticket volume
- Segment: randomized 50/50 split by student ID
- Duration: full semester minimum to capture varied use patterns

## Outcome

### Deliverables

I produced a comprehensive design package:

- Complete design system with component library
- Interactive Figma prototype (desktop and mobile)
- IA documentation with rationale
- Usability testing reports
- Implementation recommendations

### Projected Impact

Based on testing results, projected improvements:

- 40% reduction in average task completion time
- 30% reduction in portal-related support tickets
- Improved satisfaction scores across all student cohorts

## Reflection

### What Worked Well

- Card sorting provided invaluable insight into student mental models
- Iterative tree testing allowed confident IA decisions
- Focusing on top 5 tasks kept scope manageable

### Challenges

- Limited access to actual analytics required estimation
- Institutional constraints not fully understood without stakeholder access
- Technical feasibility assumptions may be optimistic

### If I Could Start Over

- Conduct stakeholder interviews earlier in the process
- Include international students more deliberately in research
- Explore voice interface possibilities

### Next Steps

This project demonstrated the value of IA-focused redesign. Potential next phases:

- Stakeholder presentation and buy-in
- Technical feasibility assessment
- Phased implementation plan
- Post-launch monitoring strategy
