## Overview

Connect Circles is an ongoing research-through-design project exploring how digital tools can facilitate authentic social connections for international students. Moving to a new country for study presents unique social challenges that existing social apps don't adequately address.

The research and synthesis phases are complete. The current public case documents the product direction, prototype scope, and validation plan rather than claiming a launched product.

## Problem

International students face significant social wellbeing challenges:

- 67% report feeling lonely in their first semester
- Language barriers complicate social interactions
- Cultural differences in socializing norms create uncertainty
- Academic pressure limits time for social activities
- Existing social apps focus on quantity over quality of connections

### Research Question

> "How might we design a digital platform that helps international students form meaningful friendships while respecting cultural differences in social norms?"

## Research

### Literature Review

I reviewed academic literature on:

- Social wellbeing and student mental health
- Cross-cultural friendship formation
- Technology-mediated social connection
- Loneliness interventions

Key theoretical frameworks informing the design:

- Weiss's Social Provisions Theory
- Self-Determination Theory (autonomy, competence, relatedness)
- Social Identity Theory

### Exploratory Interviews

I conducted semi-structured interviews with 15 international students:

- 5 from East Asian countries
- 5 from South Asian countries
- 5 from European countries

Interview topics:

- Experiences making friends in Australia
- Current strategies for meeting people
- Barriers encountered
- Role of technology in social life

### Diary Study

8 participants completed a 2-week diary study documenting:

- Social interactions (online and offline)
- Emotional states before/after interactions
- Barriers encountered
- Successful connection moments

## Insights

*Research synthesis complete. The insights below define the current product direction.*

### Insight 1: Quality Over Quantity

Participants consistently valued one deep friendship over many casual acquaintances. Current apps optimize for matching volume, not relationship depth.

### Insight 2: Shared Vulnerability

The most meaningful connections formed when both parties could be vulnerable about their "outsider" experiences. Structured sharing prompts may facilitate this.

### Insight 3: Low-Stakes First Interactions

High-pressure "coffee dates" feel stressful. Participants preferred activity-based first meetings with built-in conversation topics and natural endpoints.

### Insight 4: Cultural Context Matters

Socializing norms vary significantly. Some cultures emphasize group activities, others one-on-one. Design must accommodate diverse preferences.

## Ideation

### Design Workshop

I facilitated a co-design workshop with 8 international students exploring:

- Ideal app features and interactions
- Concerns and dealbreakers
- Cultural considerations

### Emerging Concepts

Several promising directions emerged:

**Circle Activities**
Small-group activities (3-5 people) organized around shared interests with icebreaker prompts and conversation guides.

**Vulnerability Prompts**
Structured sharing exercises that help participants open up about challenges, creating emotional bonds.

**Cultural Bridge**
Features that help users understand and navigate cultural differences in communication styles.

**Slow Matching**
Extended matching process that prioritizes compatibility over speed, with gradual information reveal.

### Next Steps

Current product direction:

- Slow matching around small-group circles instead of one-to-one discovery
- Low-fidelity prototyping of onboarding, activity prompts, and post-activity reflection
- A validation plan focused on perceived safety, cultural fit, and willingness to use over time

## Prototype

The prototype scope is defined for the next build phase.

### Planned Prototype Scope

The initial prototype should focus on:

- Onboarding flow capturing preferences and comfort levels
- Circle matching algorithm demonstration
- Activity suggestion and RSVP flow
- In-app conversation prompts
- Post-activity reflection

### Technical Considerations

Key technical decisions to explore:

- Privacy-preserving matching algorithms
- Real-time translation integration
- Location-based activity suggestions
- Moderation and safety features

## Testing

The next test should assess:

- Usability of core flows

- Cultural appropriateness across diverse users

## Reflection

### Current Learnings

- International student social needs are diverse and nuanced
- Technology can help but must be carefully designed to avoid harm
- Cultural sensitivity requires ongoing attention, not one-time consideration

### Challenges So Far

- Recruiting diverse participant pool
- Navigating sensitive topics around loneliness
- Balancing academic rigor with practical constraints

### Questions Being Explored

- What role should AI play in matching and conversation facilitation?
- How to handle safety concerns in stranger-meeting apps?
- What sustainable business model could support this service?

### Looking Ahead

This project remains an ongoing exploration of designing for social wellbeing. The next useful step is to turn the defined scope into a testable prototype and validate whether participants feel safer using a slow, small-circle model than a conventional social-discovery flow.
