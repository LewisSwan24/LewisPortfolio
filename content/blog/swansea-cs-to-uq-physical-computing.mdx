Studying Computer Science at Swansea University (2020–2024, Upper Second-Class Honours, 2:1) trains a specific reflex: before polishing an interface, ask what breaks under bad input, collisions, network loss or power loss. Writing an extended honours dissertation added a second habit: an argument holds only as far as its evidence reaches.

Moving into the Master of Interaction Design at The University of Queensland (2025–2026) at first felt like leaving determinism for messy interviews. The two trainings met once course projects put software rules onto real tables, wrists and noisy rooms.

## What desk-bound abstractions miss in the room

A desktop state machine assumes one seated person watching a monitor. Put four strangers around a tilting acrylic maze in [MazeRunners](/projects/maze), or move a Claude Code remote onto a wrist with [Walkie](/projects/walkie), and three things change:

1. **Attention goes where the body is.** At the MazeRunners exhibition (9 groups, 36 players, about 3 groups finished), our notes record players watching the ball and looking for their same-coloured teammate. The finger-sleeve vibration told them when a trap fired, and some players were startled by it the first time.
2. **Firmware timing constants are interaction rules.** The FC-51 infrared readings swung with distance, angle and lighting. A three-sample debounce and a 400 ms cooldown made one pass of the ball count once, which is what made a trap read as fair rather than random.
3. **Some friction belongs before risky actions.** [Lamplight Nook](/projects/lamplight) puts default focus on "Not now" for risky cards, [Walkie](/projects/walkie) puts approve on the front key and deny on the side key, and [Resume Autofill](/projects/autofill) stops at "Next" and never presses "Submit". Each one writes a state-machine safety rule into a habit loop.

## How I build now

UQ coursework in 2025, such as think-aloud testing and paper redraws in a team project, changed how I frame problems before any component exists. That carries into the personal builds on this site:

- **Clear ownership with AI coding tools.** On Walkie, On The Beat, Lamplight Nook and Resume Autofill, Claude Code wrote most of the code. On Grassland Iron Tide, AI tools helped with the code, copy and original assets. I set the rules, reviewed the output and verified it. Generated code comes after the state transitions and refusal limits are written down, and it is never taken as true on sight.
- **Saying which checks ran where.** For Walkie, gate H (screens and keys) passed on the real watch, and gate L ran a stand-in device through the relay. The checks of the watch working away from the cable have no run record yet, and only I have used it.
- **Bilingual parity without glossing over limits.** Writing every case in English and Chinese keeps the numbers in both versions the same.
