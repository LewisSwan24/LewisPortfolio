Across both an abandoned email triage workbench in the [Archive Vault](/vault) and [Resume Autofill](/projects/autofill), one lesson kept returning: users misread continuous confidence percentages (`0.84` vs `0.91`), yet blindly trust uniform filled fields when uncertainty is hidden.

## Why binary or percentage UI breaks operator judgment

1. **Continuous scores invite gaming or false precision**: In earlier oversight experiments, reviewers latched onto arbitrary score cutoffs rather than inspecting context—a flaw that pushed us toward discrete, honest uncertainty tiers.
2. **Guessing ambiguous inputs ruins high-stakes forms**: Filling a visa sponsorship dropdown or fabricating an open motivation essay in [Resume Autofill](/projects/autofill) is worse than skipping the field.

## Designing the three-tier tri-coded split

Instead of guessing or displaying raw floats, Autofill classifies every form input into one of three unmistakable states:

- **Tier 1 · Exact 1:1 Match (Solid border + Filled)**: Deterministic identity fields fill immediately with a side-panel audit line.
- **Tier 2 · Ambiguous Select (Dashed border + Review)**: Borderline dropdowns pause for explicit human choice.
- **Tier 3 · Open / Unknown (Double border + Blank)**: Essay prompts and unrecognized custom widgets stay intentionally empty with a clear reason logged.

Pairing border stroke geometry (`solid` / `dashed` / `double`) with text badges and side-panel explanations ensures color-blind users and dark-mode portals read every boundary without ambiguity. Compare the live sandbox in [Pattern Atlas](/patterns).
