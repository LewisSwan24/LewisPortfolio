[Resume Autofill](/projects/autofill) is the browser extension I use to fill job application forms. One rule carries most of its design: a field the tool is unsure about should look unsure, not filled. AI coding tools (Claude Code) wrote most of the code; I set the rules, reviewed the output and used it on my own applications.

## Why a percentage or a silent guess is the wrong signal

1. **A bare score invites false precision.** This is my working assumption, not a measured finding: shown `0.84` next to a field, people are more likely to pick a cutoff than to read the field. I have not run a study on it.
2. **A guessed answer on a high-stakes field is worse than a blank.** A wrong visa or work-rights answer costs far more than the minute it takes to fill the field by hand.

## Three outcomes, three line styles

Autofill sorts every field it touches into one of three visible outcomes:

- **Filled (2px solid outline)**: the field matched resume data and was filled.
- **Needs review (2px dashed outline)**: the match was uncertain, or the dropdown had no matching option. The tool stops and waits for the person.
- **Not matched or failed (3px solid outline)**: the tool could not tell what the field asks for, or the fill action failed. The field is left for the person.

Fields for which the resume holds no data get no outline at all.

The states differ in line weight and style, not only in colour, so they stay readable for colour-blind users and on dark pages. Compare the sandbox in [Pattern Atlas](/patterns).
