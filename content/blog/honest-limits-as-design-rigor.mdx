Too many case studies borrow the visual grammar of finished consumer launches for what were actually student prototypes, personal utilities, or architectural experiments. "Increased efficiency by 42%" sounds polished on a slide deck until a hiring engineer asks for the baseline script or cohort size.

Across the six public works in this portfolio, the fastest way to make a design decision credible was to publish the exact ceiling where testing stopped.

## Separation between local proof and unmeasured field claims

Take three prototypes built under completely different verification regimes:

- **[Grassland Iron Tide](/projects/grassland)** passes **430/430** unit tests across its unified five-mission command grammar and 20 Hz checkpoint restoration chain alongside two-resolution desktop playthroughs. That proves rule determinism and local RC stability, not how outside players onboard unassisted on the public URL.
- **[MazeRunners](/projects/maze)** ran live during public exhibition play at The University of Queensland across **9 groups (36 people, roughly 3 groups finishing)** wired with 10 debounced FC-51 IR sensors and vibration sleeves. That validates physical co-touch pressure and noisy-room visibility, while the handwritten score sheet stayed unarchived without channel telemetry.
- **[On The Beat](/projects/onthebeat)** verifies real dual-wristband mutual `MEET` state over an always-on Sydney Fly.io relay and dual-boot partition isolation alongside Walkie, while explicitly noting zero live concert wearer studies conducted to date.

Treating CI test counts, single-venue group observation, and bench-verified hardware loops as interchangeable "production KPIs" destroys signal for both human and automated readers.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| [Grassland Iron Tide](/projects/grassland) | 430/430 deterministic tests + 5-mission two-resolution local RC playthrough | Live production load-time audit & first-time outside player learnability | Hosted URL telemetry run across all 5 chapter loads + novice tutorial probe |

## Why printable dossiers and the AI Workbench foreground failure modes

When we added the A4 Editorial Critique dossier (`/brief/print`) and expanded the [Rationale AI Lab](/ai), every exported sheet placed **Human Tension**, **Rejected Alternatives**, and **Honest Scope & Limits** front and center beside an offline-safe vector QR code pointing directly to the live interactive prototype or case record. And when a recruiter asks our BM25 workbench a question outside verified records, the endpoint responds transparently with `Honest Limit` gaps rather than fabricating missing telemetry.

Naming what has *not* been proven is not modesty; it is how a design engineer prevents teams from mistaking prototype confidence for production guarantees.
