Hardware prototypes rarely stay on isolated benches. [Walkie](/projects/walkie) and [On The Beat](/projects/onthebeat) share the 8 MB flash of one M5StickS3: On The Beat lives in `app0` and Walkie in `app1`. That constraint made embedded safety an ergonomic problem before anyone presses a button. Claude Code wrote most of the code for both projects, including the guard below; I set the rules, reviewed the output and checked the results on the watch.

## Why dual-boot slots fail quietly

1. **Some build targets write outside the app's own partition**: a full erase or a filesystem upload aimed at one app can wipe the other.
2. **The wrong app can look like a broken one**: if the watch boots into the other app after a restart, a tester can mistake it for a network fault.

## A guard on the build, a deliberate switch on the watch

`firmware/guard.py` runs before every Walkie build and refuses the `erase`, `uploadfs` and `uploadfsota` PlatformIO targets by name, because each would write outside `app1`. It does not parse addresses; it simply blocks those targets and points to the project's own flash and boot tools.

On the watch, switching apps is deliberate: both keys held for 3 seconds open a switch confirmation, and only a front-key press then sets the boot partition and restarts. See the [Walkie case](/projects/walkie) for the device checks.
