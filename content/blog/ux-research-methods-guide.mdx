## Introduction

As interaction designers, we often face the challenge of understanding our users deeply before diving into solutions. The research method you choose can significantly impact the quality of insights you gather and, ultimately, the success of your design.

In this guide, I'll walk through the most effective UX research methods I've used in my projects, including when to use each and practical tips for getting the best results.

## Qualitative vs. Quantitative Research

Before choosing a method, understand the type of data you need:

- **Qualitative**: Rich, descriptive insights about *why* users behave a certain way
- **Quantitative**: Numerical data about *what* and *how many* users do something

Most successful projects use a mix of both.

## Core Research Methods

### 1. User Interviews

**When to use**: Early discovery phase, understanding user mental models

User interviews are my go-to method for deep understanding. They allow you to:

- Explore user motivations and pain points
- Understand context and environment
- Build empathy with your target audience

**Pro tip**: Prepare a discussion guide but stay flexible. The best insights often come from unexpected tangents.

### 2. Usability Testing

**When to use**: Evaluating prototypes or existing products

Usability testing is essential for validating design decisions. I typically conduct:

- **Moderated tests** for complex flows where I need to probe deeper
- **Unmoderated tests** for quick validation with larger sample sizes

In my Spectra Lens project, usability testing helped us achieve a 92% task success rate, up from 75% in early iterations.

### 3. Contextual Inquiry

**When to use**: Understanding real-world usage patterns

Observing users in their natural environment reveals insights you'd never get from interviews alone. I used this method extensively in the Connect Circles project to understand how families communicate across distances.

### 4. Surveys

**When to use**: Validating hypotheses at scale

Surveys work best when you know what to ask. Use them to:

- Validate qualitative findings quantitatively
- Segment your user base
- Track satisfaction over time

## Choosing the Right Method

Consider these factors:

1. **Project phase**: Discovery → Interviews/Contextual Inquiry; Validation → Usability Testing
2. **Time and budget**: Unmoderated tests and surveys are more efficient
3. **Research questions**: Open-ended questions need qualitative methods

## Key Takeaways

- Start with qualitative research to build understanding
- Validate with quantitative methods before major decisions
- Always triangulate findings from multiple sources
- Document and share insights with your team

Research isn't a checkbox—it's an ongoing conversation with your users that should inform every design decision.
