Most bilingual portfolios treat the second language as an afterthought: ship English pages first, batch-translate copy weeks later, and hope metrics, dates, and honest caveats never go out of sync. Within two edits, the Chinese page promises something the English page already retracted.

## Structural parity enforced at schema level

In this portfolio — as well as in [Lamplight Nook](/projects/lamplight) where every café commission card follows system EN/ZH locale — bilingual parity lives inside the data contract rather than manual spreadsheets:

1. **Co-located `LocalizedString` fields**: Every case study decision (`CaseRecordSchema`), deep interaction module (`CaseModulesRecordSchema`), and [Pattern Atlas](/patterns) entry stores `{ en, zh }` side-by-side inside the exact same TypeScript object.
2. **Server-only modular loading**: Large bilingual datasets under `src/data/cases/modules/` and `src/data/patterns/` stay strictly on the server while `src/messages/{en,zh}.json` carries only lightweight UI chrome labels — keeping client hydration bundles small.
3. **Automated Vitest & Playwright parity gates**: `src/lib/__tests__/blog.test.ts` fails CI if any essay slug or `date` diverges across locales, if an English title leaks Han characters, or if a Chinese essay falls back to untranslated English. Meanwhile `e2e/crawler.spec.ts` fails if any `/zh/blog/` internal link accidentally drops its `/zh/` prefix.

## Writing the nuance natively, never machine-translating idioms

Because English technical brevity and Chinese structural phrasing follow different rhythms, keeping `{ en, zh }` adjacent lets both versions stay crisp and faithful to the exact same evidence boundary — never inflating sample sizes on one side while hedging on the other.
