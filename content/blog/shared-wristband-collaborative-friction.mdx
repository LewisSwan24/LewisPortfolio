Give four strangers four independent buttons around a physical game and they will often play silently in parallel. Wire each color axis so no single player can tilt the board alone, and physical conductivity becomes an immediate social mechanic.

## Three field lessons from 36 exhibition players

Across 9 public groups (36 players, ~3 completions) on [MazeRunners](/projects/maze), observing real bodies around the wooden table overturned three studio assumptions:

1. **Sleeve fitting became an onboarding ritual**: Visitors struggled to slip on the vibration finger sleeves solo, so facilitator-assisted fitting naturally kicked off conversation before Round 1 even started.
2. **Players never looked at the score screen**: Eyes stayed locked on the rolling steel ball—meaning finger-sleeve haptics and the board buzzer carried 100% of real-time penalty communication.
3. **Sensor debounce protects table morale**: Without our `45 ms` FC-51 stability window and `1200 ms` zone lockout (inspectable in the [Protocol Replay Studio](/studio/replay?track=maze)), a wobbling ball double-fired penalties and broke group trust immediately.

## Carrying social friction into wearable gigs

The same insight—designing hardware to pull attention back to the room rather than the glass screen—shaped [On The Beat](/projects/onthebeat), where Card 3 intentionally dims out after matching.
