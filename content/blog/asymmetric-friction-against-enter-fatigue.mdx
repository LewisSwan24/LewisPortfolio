Standard desktop UX treats symmetry as polish: *"Cancel"* on the left, a bright *"Continue"* button focused by default on the right. That symmetry works for saving a draft — and fails dangerously when autonomous agents ask to overwrite files or execute shell commands.

After watching my own daily workflows in [Lamplight Nook](/projects/lamplight), [Walkie](/projects/walkie), and [Resume Autofill](/projects/autofill), I stopped treating friction as an enemy and started designing **asymmetric friction**: cheap to pause, deliberate to commit.

## Making refusal the path of least resistance

1. **Default-Safe Focus Lock ([Lamplight Nook](/projects/lamplight))**: Before releasing v1.0 on 5 November 2026, a dedicated *"Safety before 1.0"* pass inverted every risky confirmation card. Initial keyboard focus and primary visual weight land strictly on **Not now**. Pressing `Enter` or `Escape` out of habit pauses the step cleanly without discarding blackboard progress, while staff work exclusively on duplicated file copies.
2. **Tactile Key Asymmetry ([Walkie](/projects/walkie))**: Speech capture uses holding the Front Key, yet voice transcripts can never authorize a shell command. Tool permissions pause on the watch screen until a deliberate Front Key press (approve) or Side Key press (deny, with optional spoken reason).
3. **Human Submit Lock ([Resume Autofill](/projects/autofill))**: On Beisen and Moka campus portals, *"Submit"* frequently occupies the exact screen slot where *"Next"* stood on earlier pages. The extension advances intermediate pages cleanly, splits uncertain fields into dashed/double borders, and refuses to touch the final Submit control.

## Friction that preserves agency without lecturing

Asymmetric friction never scolds the user with three confirmation popups. It simply ensures that reflexively pressing `Enter` or glancing away produces the safest reversible outcome. Test all three patterns interactively in the [Pattern Atlas](/patterns).
