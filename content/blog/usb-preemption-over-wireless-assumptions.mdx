Every wireless-first gadget demo promises seamless handoff between desk and sofa. In practice, mid-utterance channel stitching between USB Serial and WebSocket relays is where subtle duplicate tool invocations are born.

## Deterministic preemption without silent stitching

In [Walkie](/projects/walkie), hardware transport arbitration follows two crisp rules verified on silicon and inspectable frame by frame inside the [Protocol Replay Studio](/studio/replay?track=walkie):

1. **USB DTR immediately preempts `wss://`**: Plugging in USB-C at the desk switches capture to raw `16 kHz` PCM over CDC serial with zero watch menu diving.
2. **Mid-turn cable swaps abort cleanly**: Pulling or plugging the USB cable during an active hold immediately drops the partial audio buffer and notifies the operator (`Link switched — release and retry`) instead of splicing half an ADPCM turn onto a half-recorded PCM stream.

## Why honest interruption beats brittle continuity

Users recover from a 1-second explicit retry cue effortlessly; they do not recover easily when a spliced audio buffer fires a half-parsed shell command. Honest state transitions preserve operator trust across every physical transition.
