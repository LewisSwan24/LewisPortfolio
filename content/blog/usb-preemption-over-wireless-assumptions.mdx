Wireless-first gadget demos promise seamless handoff between desk and sofa. My concern is that stitching one spoken turn across USB serial and a WebSocket relay is exactly where half-heard commands and duplicate tool calls could come from.

## Preemption without silent stitching

In [Walkie](/projects/walkie), the bridge follows two rules, documented in the project README. Claude Code wrote most of Walkie's code; I set the rules, reviewed the output and verified what is marked as verified below.

1. **The cable comes first**: the bridge looks for the watch on USB first and uses the relay only when there is no cable and a pairing key and relay host are set. While on the relay it looks for the cable every 3 seconds and moves to it when it appears. Over the cable, audio goes as `16 kHz` PCM by default.
2. **A move abandons the turn**: moving between cable and relay is not seamless. A turn in progress is dropped, as on any lost link, rather than splicing half a turn from one link onto the other.

**What has been checked:** whole turns over USB have run on the watch. The on-device check of moving between relay and cable (gate M) has not been run yet, so the second rule is designed and documented but not verified on the watch. The [Protocol Replay Studio](/studio/replay?track=walkie) steps through a hand-authored simulation of it.

## Why honest interruption beats brittle continuity

My reasoning, not a measured result: saying a turn again costs a few seconds, while a spliced recording that reaches an agent as a half-formed shell command can cost much more. Dropping the turn is the cheaper failure.
