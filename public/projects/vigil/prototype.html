V I G I L

You are the last check before the inbox.

Meridian Group's mail gateway holds suspicious messages in quarantine and attaches a classifier's verdict to each one. Your job is not to sort mail. It is to check the classifier's work.

It is right most of the time, which is the problem. It misses real attacks and it holds harmless mail, and both cost something — a released phishing email takes a colleague's account, a blocked real one loses work that mattered.

What counts as phishing

Any message where the sender is pretending to be someone they are not. That includes messages with no link, no attachment and no request in them yet: an attacker opens with small talk to establish a thread before asking for anything. If the identity is false, the message is phishing.

Reading the headers

Each message shows its authentication results. Those verify the sending server, never the person — mail sent from a real Gmail account passes every check while claiming to be from anyone at all.

Your four options

accept  A
The classifier got it right. Apply its verdict.
override  O
It got it wrong. Apply the opposite verdict.
escalate  E
You cannot tell. Send it to the security team.
skip  S
Leave it in the queue and move on.

Z undoes your last decision. Nothing here touches a real mailbox — every message is fabricated for this study.
